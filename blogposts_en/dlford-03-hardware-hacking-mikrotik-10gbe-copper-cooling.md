(don't forget the solder too!)&#xA; - <a href=&#34;https://amzn.to/48a0L2G&#34; target=&#34;_blank&#34; rel=&#34;noopener noreferrer&#34;>[Affiliate Link]</a></p>&#xA;<p>&#xA;Heat shrink tubing.&#xA; - <a href=&#34;https://amzn.to/49hwwYL&#34; target=&#34;_blank&#34; rel=&#34;noopener noreferrer&#34;>[Affiliate Link]</a></p>&#xA;<p>&#xA;A small heat gun (optional, you could just use a lighter for the heat shrink tubing).&#xA; - <a href=&#34;https://amzn.to/3WSzmMs&#34; target=&#34;_blank&#34; rel=&#34;noopener noreferrer&#34;>[Affiliate Link]</a></p>&#xA;<h2 id=&#34;disassembly&#34;>Disassembly</h2>&#xA;<p>First, unplug your switch! Then you can take out the screws along the top edge of the top cover. With the top cover off, remove the seven screws holding the PCB in place.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;PCB screw locations, 1 in each of the four corners, one slightly inset from the front left corner, and two on the large heatsink in the middle.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-pcb-screws.a958199f373524fa8c6233dfa3bee1b61e38a48a478e39ad56225d6e071ee48f_hu_c1c0455870d88b38.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p>Lift the back end of the PCB up and slide it toward the back of the case, the PCB should lift out easily after that.</p>&#xA;<h2 id=&#34;heatsinks&#34;>Heatsinks</h2>&#xA;<p>These are peal and stick, using three of the large 14x14mm ones fits perfectly if you start from the back end of the SFP+ cage while leaving enough room for the cage to slot back into the case. In the photo I&rsquo;m using a different arrangement of heatsinks for the inner (right side) SFP+ cage, because I&rsquo;m doing this to two switches and didn&rsquo;t have enough of the large ones to do both.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Heatsinks applied to SFP&#43; cages&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-sfp-heatsinks.013a945b621e7c0bea0a1ce786ea04452cf8112b3aeccb2f49eb524c92877fb2_hu_a59c7ef8c5eb4da2.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p>Put the PCB aside for now, as we&rsquo;ll be working on the empty case next. If you intend to spray paint the case now is the best time to do that.</p>&#xA;<h2 id=&#34;fan-mounting&#34;>Fan Mounting</h2>&#xA;<p>The case already has a mounting place for a 30mm fan, but Noctua doesn&rsquo;t (to my knowledge) make a 30mm fan, so we&rsquo;ll need to make some modifications.</p>&#xA;<p>First, put the lid on the empty case and hold the fan up to it to mark where we&rsquo;ll need to cut the lid with a pencil or pen.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Case with lid marked for cutting.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-lid-marked.fd56d945a1097f51665fdddef9d9288c234b52a78075cecce6129062cfde865b_hu_f7782c31f48c3a80.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p>Use a metal cut-off wheel to make room for the fan on the lid of the case, just outside of the marked lines. Then using a sanding bit to clean up any rough edges. Then put the lid back on the empty case and hold the fan up again to mark the screw holes and the cut-out for the fan blades. The existing fan cut-out will be too restrictive for a 40mm fan and probably have a negative effect on noise levels too, so we&rsquo;ll be just cutting a large round hole for maximum airflow.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Case marked for cutting.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-case-marked.d37ad35f81a0d627b9d53b161af3811a2e75dad16219bd80eb9faf32eef1fa79_hu_aeb866f267daf08d.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p>Use the cut-off wheel again for the large hole, and a 3/16" drill bit for the screw holes. Again, using a sanding bit to clean up any rough edges.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Case with fan holes cut.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-case-cut.ed22155b758ea31b19f34896c240b1c5bb19bd9a5bc6ec6e52cd6fcbac55908b_hu_2b373efaa851c0d5.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p>Now the fan can be installed with the fan screws, make sure it&rsquo;s oriented to pull air out the back of the case.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Fan mounted to case.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-fan-mounted.9fb700d01390e3450c279c252ffc80c89548345538d603f15b1cb524941327e7_hu_10b85cd1f960e942.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p>Next, we&rsquo;ll need to cut the end of the connector off of the wiring for the fan, and pull off the heat shrink covering the wires. We only need the yellow (+24v) and black (ground) wires, put some heat shrink around the wires and route them through one of the air holes on the back of the case.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Fan wires routed into the case.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-wires-routed.d69f9b88531b40162ad50546b3d7c8c070487556823f8ea4259a3d5f7b59735a_hu_e579b633567a0561.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p>Now we can put the wire-wrap back on the wires inside the case and use more heat shrink tubing to secure it.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Wires wrapped and routed through the case.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-wire-wrapped.38528b80137a931e12136d7aec33b9e85059bd14b8e0c0d6fd06a659d1f68e11_hu_85687980c5e31185.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><h2 id=&#34;airflow-control&#34;>Airflow Control</h2>&#xA;<p>Since this case was designed to be fanless, we&rsquo;ll need to cover up some of the ventilation holes so the fresh air comes in from the front of the case to properly cool all the components. We&rsquo;ll use some foil tape to block off most of the existing air paths.</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Foil tape placement.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-airflow-1.bcf1628d69f8a9b5113327d2737f0daf1759458c98c0c974e3b6cfab165245b8_hu_50db5bbf386b0c4e.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Foil tape placement.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-airflow-2.619e0bfd99c9908c3edb57114d5b136577c5f04565722ec1a41003034e7937a8_hu_aed3dbc0ae1f6abe.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Foil tape placement.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-airflow-3.7e48bada6293cde01e6f54a73a5375bc6982051c26a914bd9a1c98d6889e9d45_hu_317c0aace7dc01a8.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><h2 id=&#34;fan-wiring&#34;>Fan Wiring</h2>&#xA;<p>Now we can solder the fan wires to the PCB, there is direct power from the power supply at J2 (right next to the power input socket), with the hole closest to the power input socket being positive (yellow wire).</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;Fan wires soldered to PCB at J2.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;452&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-soldered.8e8b20ec9b7767413bda4df55e0c6fce2e51a9f6a7a552664110c056b329a8bd_hu_37b9d9fc6dfb3606.jpg&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p><p>With that done, you can re-install the PCB into the case (same as disassembly but in reverse), and put everything back together.</p>&#xA;<h2 id=&#34;results&#34;>Results</h2>&#xA;<p>Before the modification, CPU temps were in the high 70s and the SFP+ module would overheat to 95C and be shut off for safety within a few minutes. Now, CPU temps are in the mid 50s and the SFP+ module usually sits around the high 60s or low 70s. I would consider that great success!</p>&#xA;<p></p>&#xA;<img&#xA; alt=&#34;A screenshot of the status showing the SFP&#43; module at 67C and CPU at 54C.&#34;&#xA; &#xA; &#xA; width=&#34;600&#34;&#xA; height=&#34;378&#34;&#xA; src=&#34;https://www.dlford.io/media/rss/mikrotik-10gbe-copper-cooling-mod-results.1ab3b216d382f4968f2f0a72ff9693330bcc390355936c982c5163f2e7813534_hu_53f55aaee1ccaf55.png&#34;&#xA;/>&#xA;<p></p>
